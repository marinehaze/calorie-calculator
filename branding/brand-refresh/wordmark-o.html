Piatto Wordmark · the arc as the final “o” · three refinements, no redesign

One word, one opening. How much of the “o” becomes the arc?

Every version is drawn from the typeface’s own “o”: same advance width, same ink box, same stroke, measured from Plus Jakarta Sans 600 at render time. Only the opening changes. The name is written “Piatto” everywhere in text and in accessibility labels; the arc is a visual treatment of the wordmark only.

Recommendation · readability and distinctiveness only

Take B. It keeps the plate and gives the letter back.

  • A is the most distinctive and reads as a “c” or a broken ring before it reads as an “o”, especially at 20 px and below where the round terminals close the gap unevenly.
  • B holds the word as “piatto” at every size shown, and the opening is still unmistakably a portion taken from a whole. The uniform stroke keeps the terminals round and clean when reversed.
  • C is the most faithful letter, but at 48 px and below the opening collapses into what looks like a rendering flaw in an ordinary “o”. The distinctiveness is gone before the readability gain matters.
