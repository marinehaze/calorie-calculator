Calorie & Nutrition App — Mobile

Stage 02 / Branding · Stylescape · Round 01

Three visual directions for “I know exactly what I’m eating.”

One brand territory — technological, precise, minimal, confident — explored three ways. Each direction is built around the same conviction: the nutrition number is the product. Everything else exists to make that number legible, trustworthy and fast to read. The three are not one design in three accent colours; they differ in colour personality, typography, grid, density and how measurement itself is drawn.

Direction 01 Dark base · Signal amber · Circular gauge

01

Instrument

Readout, not decoration.

The app behaves like a measuring instrument rather than a lifestyle companion. A near-black panel, a single luminous readout, and calibration marks that admit the number was measured — not guessed. Hairlines divide, nothing encloses.

Amber is the only light source on the panel, so the calorie figure is literally the brightest thing on screen. Every supporting value is typeset in monospace at a fraction of its size, the way a lab display separates result from parameters.

Palette

PrimaryPanel Black#0A0B0D
SurfaceGraphite#14171B
SecondaryReadout White#F1F4F6
SupportTitanium#98A1AA
AccentSignal Amber#FF8A00

Why amber. Amber is the colour of instrument readouts, calibration marks and night-legible gauges — it carries an association with measurement, not with food or wellness. It is warm enough to keep a dark interface humane at 21:00, and it is the single highest-luminance element in the system, so attention lands on the number by physics rather than by styling. Deliberately not green: nothing here should suggest a diet product.

Grid, density & photography

Photography tone reference — not final imagery

Grid. 12 columns, 8 px base unit, visible hairlines at column boundaries in dense areas. Alignment is stated, not implied.

Density. High. This direction can hold a multi-item dish with eight rows and a running total without feeling crowded — the hairlines do the separating that whitespace would otherwise have to.

Photography. One image per screen, maximum. Single warm key light, deep falloff into the panel black, hard corner crop marks. The food emerges from the same darkness the UI sits on, so photography never becomes a second background.

Avoids. Glow effects, soft gradients, rounded cards, coloured macro trios, any circle that isn't measuring something.

01 — Strongest at night and in the Nutrition Result screen, where a single dominant number is the entire point. The most “device-like” of the three.

Direction 02 Warm paper base · Ultramarine · Ledger progress

02

Ledger

Every gram accounted for.

Nutrition as a precise account. The visual language is the ruled book, the index card and the technical drawing: horizontal rules that carry information, figures right-aligned so they stack into a readable column, and a double rule under anything that totals.

Where Instrument is a device, Ledger is a record. That shifts the emotional register from monitoring to bookkeeping — calm, literate, unhurried — which is exactly the tone for a product about awareness rather than restriction. Paper white, ink black, one blue for entries.

Palette

PrimaryArchive Paper#F5F2EC
SurfaceSheet#FFFDF8
SecondaryInk#14161A
SupportRule#D8D2C4
AccentUltramarine#1F3BE0

Why ultramarine on warm paper. This pair belongs to blueprints, library stamps, ledger ink and technical annotation — records kept carefully by someone. The warmth of the paper (#F5F2EC, not white) keeps a data-dense screen from feeling clinical, while true ultramarine is cold, saturated and entirely free of food or wellness association. It marks entries and targets only; it never decorates.

Grid, density & photography

Photography tone reference — not final imagery

Grid. A baseline grid before a column grid. Type sits on a 4 px baseline and every rule falls on it, so rules read as ruling rather than as borders.

Density. Medium, with generous leading. More air than Instrument, but the air is between rows, not around cards — there are no cards.

Photography. Small, catalogued, fixed 1:1 plates at a consistent size — reference images in an index, flat and evenly lit, slightly warm. Used in recipe ingredient lists for recognition, never full-bleed, always with a rule and a caption beneath.

Avoids. Shadows, cards, gradients, coloured backgrounds. Hierarchy comes from rule weight and figure alignment only.

02 — Strongest on Recipe Detail and multi-item dishes, where ingredients, amounts and per-serving nutrition all want to be read as one accounted list. The most literate and the most printable of the three.

Direction 03 Cool concrete base · Hi-vis marking · Counted units

03

Matrix

Everything is countable.

Food resolved into discrete units. Nothing here is drawn as a continuous curve or a smooth bar — quantities are counted in cells, because a count is something you can verify by looking. One cell is 22 kcal; seventy-three of them are filled.

The result is a system where measurement is the graphic language itself. A hard 8 px module governs every edge, the base is cool poured-concrete grey, and exactly one hi-vis colour marks the measured part. It is the most graphic and the most contemporary of the three.

Palette

PrimaryConcrete#E9EBEC
Unit cellCast Grey#C7CDD1
SecondaryCarbon#101316
SupportSlate#5C646B
AccentHi-Vis#D6F32B

Why hi-vis on concrete. #D6F32B is a marking colour — safety tape, calibration marks, highlighter, test equipment. It is the exact opposite of the sage and mint greens that signal “wellness”: it is aggressive, synthetic and industrial, and it appears only on the part of a value that has actually been measured. Against cold neutral greys it never reads as nature. Because it is the only saturated element in the system, filled always means counted.

No arc, no bar. The day is a hundred units and you can count what is left — the carbon cell marks the current position. Overshoot adds a second short row below the grid in carbon rather than turning anything red, so exceeding a target is shown, not scolded.

The same matrix works at three sizes: 100 cells for a day, 10 cells for a meal, a single cell as an inline unit marker in a list.

Grid, density & photography

Photography tone reference — not final imagery

Grid. An 8 px module made visible. Everything is a whole number of cells wide; nothing floats between them. The grid is the composition, not a guide behind it.

Density. Airy but rigid — large empty modules are allowed, half-modules are not. The most graphic of the three and the one that reads best at a glance.

Photography. Hard 1:1 crops occupying exactly one module, cool and evenly lit, shot from directly above. An image is a tile in the matrix like any other tile; a hi-vis bar beneath it ties it to the system.

Avoids. Rounded corners, shadows, gradients, any curve at all. Curvature would break the premise that everything is a discrete unit.

03 — Strongest on Recipe Discovery, where cards, filters and food images all need to sit on one visible module. The most distinctive at a distance, and the most demanding to keep disciplined.
